§ 01

The Section

What this is, and the one thing that makes it different from everything adjacent to it.

Most writing about sport is done from the surface — by people describing a thing they have never done at speed, under pressure, on the day it counted.

Scarpline is the other approach. Every unit below is built from inside its subject: swimming written by someone who races it at regional and international level, software built by the person who needed it to exist, books written rather than assembled. The units are ordered by depth — how long each has been forming — not by how loudly it announces itself. Recent work sits near the surface. The slow work sits in the basement, where it belongs.

Nothing here claims to be finished. A section is a record of what has accumulated so far.

01

Jarvis

Interface Built
Voice-driven Three personas Hands-free Light / Heavy tiers

What it is

A command interface you talk to. Three personas share one model but hold the conversation differently. A live radar, an orb that responds to speech, and a microphone with three deliberate states — open, standby, and released — so the recording light actually goes out when you hand the mic back.

How it works

Light tier answers quickly. Heavy tier is for deep work — the interface darkens, output ceilings rise, and it's instructed to deliver complete files rather than truncate silently. It searches the live web, draws 3D blueprints you can orbit and export as .obj, pulls weather, and opens real installed apps by URL scheme.

The detail worth knowing

Interruption is a first-class feature. A button, a tap on the orb, the Escape key, or simply saying stop will cut the voice mid-word and abort the request in flight so it isn't burning tokens. Pause holds instead of cancelling, and resumes exactly where it stopped.

Records: deployed · relay function required · spend limit strongly advised

02

The Forge

Engine Built
Long-work engine 200,000 char ceiling 60 parts max Self-calibrating

The problem it solves

No single model response can hold a book. Every model has a per-reply ceiling, so a request for a whole novel gets cut off mid-sentence and pretends it didn't. Any tool claiming otherwise is chaining calls behind the scenes without telling you.

How it works

It writes the way a person does — plans a chapter outline, then writes part after part, stitching them into one continuous document. It does not resend the growing manuscript on every call; only the outline plus the tail of what it just wrote. The seams stay invisible and the cost stops ballooning as the book grows.

The detail worth knowing

It discovers its own ceiling. If the account rejects a part as too large, it halves the budget and continues rather than dying. Tested against a simulated rejection: it recovered to a working budget and finished cleanly.

Honest note: chapters are naturally independent, so prose forges well. Long codebases depend on cross-references a part-by-part writer loses track of — review those module by module.

Streamlyne

Publication — Swimming In progress
Written from inside Race analysis Taper logs Technique video

What it is

Swimming written by someone who swims it. Taper logs including the middle weeks that feel terrible. What a coach actually changed in a stroke and the split it produced. The ninety minutes before a final. Race analysis of real swims, with the footage.

What it is not

Ten tips for better freestyle. That page exists ten thousand times and every version was written by someone who has never held a taper. If a fitness blogger could have written it, it doesn't go here.

Method

Search-targeted pieces answer the question in the first two sentences — mobile readers leave when the answer is buried under an anecdote. Insider pieces don't chase rankings; they exist so a reader who arrives once decides to stay.

04

Splits

Tools — Free In progress
Free, no signup Yards ↔ metres Shareable URLs Wet-hands tested

What it is

Pace and interval calculators built for the one place they get used: a wet phone on a pool deck between sets. Convert across distances and units, generate sets from a target pace and rest interval, and work out the per-50 splits a goal time actually demands.

Constraints, non-negotiable

Free with no signup — a wall would destroy the search traffic the tool exists to catch. Large tap targets. No page reloads, no spinners; it calculates on input. Every calculation gets its own URL, so a swimmer sharing a set with a teammate builds a link back without being asked.

Queued behind it

SWOLF calculator, taper countdown, meet split predictor, training volume tracker — in that order, built only once the first one is genuinely being used.

05

Books

Publishing In progress
The Forbidden Scales 20 main volumes 15 special stories Four authors

The first title

The Forbidden Scales — an epic dragon saga of loyalty, ancient power, and the bonds forged between unlikely companions. Twenty main volumes and fifteen companion stories, written by four voices in one world. Each author owns their dragon, their companion, and their corner of the story.

Why it holds together

Thirteen Shard bloodlines mapped across a Wheel of Correspondence, where every opposition is reasoned rather than decorative — Wind against Crystal is mobility against durability, and the tactical consequence follows. The Galaxy Shard sits outside the Wheel entirely, appearing not as a chosen one but as a correction when the system stops turning.

The constraint that keeps it honest

The project runs on written-down Laws — no omnipotent characters, no wish-magic, body limits apply, four pages minimum per chapter. Rules set in advance are what separate worldbuilding that holds from worldbuilding that collapses in book six.

Co-authored: Elina Yoon · Daniel Lim · Raven Taylor · Varsha Gangu

Reviews

Gear — Tested Planned
Trained in, not unboxed Disclosure at top Goggles · Suits · Watches

The rule

Nothing is reviewed that hasn't been swum in. Not worn once for a photograph — trained in, across a real block, in the conditions it's meant for. A swimmer can tell within two sentences whether the writer has actually used the goggles.

On affiliate money

Every affiliate relationship is disclosed at the top of the page, not buried in a footer. Legally required, but that isn't the reason — a sophisticated audience trusts transparent disclosure more than they trust silence, and the trust is the entire business.

Analysis — Multi-sport Planned
Sport logic Beyond swimming Numbers over folklore

What it is

The same method applied past swimming. Why a tactic works and under what conditions it stops working. What a split actually tells you versus what people say it tells you. Where received wisdom in a sport turns out to be inherited rather than tested.

§ 03

The Laws

Constraints written down in advance, so they can't be quietly negotiated away later when they become inconvenient. No exceptions have been granted.

LAW I
Written from inside, or not written

If the writer hasn't done the thing at speed, under pressure, on a day it counted — the piece doesn't run. Secondhand expertise is the one commodity already in oversupply.

LAW II
Tools stay free

No signup wall, no trial, no account required. A calculator behind a form is a lead magnet pretending to be a tool, and swimmers can tell the difference immediately.

LAW III
Disclose at the top

Every affiliate relationship stated before the review begins, never in a footer. If a disclosure would embarrass the piece, the piece is the problem.

LAW IV
Nothing described as finished that isn't

Planned means planned. In progress means in progress. Every status on this page is accurate on the day it was written, including the unflattering ones.

LAW V
Depth over frequency

Publishing more is easy and worth very little. A unit stays in the log because it accumulated something, not because it filled a slot on a calendar.

LAW VI
Keep the failures visible

Wrong calls stay published with a note, not deleted. The archive is load-bearing — everything above it is more credible because it's there.

§ 04

Depth Key

Depth is not decoration. It records how long a unit has been forming — not its importance, not its revenue, not how recently it was mentioned. Recent work sits near the surface because it hasn't had time to become anything yet.

0–030 m · Recent Built and working, but young. Still changing shape week to week.
031–070 m · Forming Actively accumulating. In progress, with the shape becoming clear.
071–130 m · Bedded Settled into a defined form. Committed to, whether or not it has launched.
131–190 m · Deep Long-horizon work. Measured in years, not publishing cycles.
191 m+ · Basement The foundation everything rests on. Slowest to form, last to change.
